The twelve-month plan

Four phases, sequenced by geography rather than by task, because each band of markets has to be earned before the next becomes reachable. The demand figure on each phase is what that phase opens up; the running total is everything reachable by the end of it.

Markets: Rome, Cave Spring

  • Consolidate brands., seniors. and blog.studio-kate.com into one site. The first two rank for nothing; the blog holds the only 85 rankings you own, so its content moves with a mapped redirect for every URL.
  • Rebuild the senior outfit posts on the main site. They already sit at positions 44 to 67 for terms searched around 8,100 times a month nationally.
  • Build the headshot, LinkedIn headshot and brand session service pages.
  • Review the Google Business Profile category against what you can evidence.

Quick wins available now: 8 Rome terms sit one position from the top, and 3 of those carry measurable demand worth 2,130 searches a month between them. Seven are held by the same competitor, so one fix moves the whole cluster rather than seven separate ones.

Cedartown is the second quick win and it is already half won. You appear in 11 of 26 searches there, including a third place for headshot photo and a fourth for headshots, from 17.8 miles away with no deliberate effort. It is the only market outside Rome where you hold a top-three placement at all.

The senior blog posts are the third. They already rank between positions 44 and 67 for terms searched around 8,100 times a month nationally, stranded on a subdomain that passes none of that strength to the main site. Moving them is a migration, not a writing project.

Building trust: Get the review engine running: a request goes out after every session. You are at 32 reviews and the business beating you on headshots has 28, so this is a gap you can close inside a quarter.

What success looks like: Take headshots and professional headshots in Rome. You are second and third on both today.

What the data says not to do

  • Do not build a page per town for business terms. Searches pairing a town with "brand photographer", "business headshots" or "corporate headshots" measured essentially zero in all 31 markets. Those pages would target searches that do not exist, and a stack of near-identical location pages is the pattern Google treats as doorway content.
  • Do not build the strategy on "brand photographer". It is under 10 searches a month in every county you serve. It is a good description of the work and a poor way to be found.
  • Do not expect the content retainer to be found through search. Tested three separate ways, including creator-economy vocabulary, and it does not register. It is a real service people buy, just not one they search for. It gets sold in the room.
How this was measured: each search was run live against Google's local results at depth 20, one search per town per term, with no estimation.
