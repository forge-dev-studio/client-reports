The search terms, and what each one is worth

Search volume here is your market area only — the counties you actually serve, from Floyd and Chattooga through Cobb and Cherokee up to Hamilton County in Tennessee, plus the five north-metro suburbs you reach. It deliberately excludes the rest of Georgia, because Savannah, Augusta and Macon are not markets you can sell into and counting them would flatter every number on this page. Difficulty estimates how hard the first page is to break into, based on the strength of the sites already ranking: under 10 is winnable on content alone, over 30 usually needs authority built first.

Why some rows say "under 10 a month". Google reports a minimum of 10 searches per month and rounds hard below that, so a term genuinely searched two or three times in a county comes back as 10 — or as nothing. Where every county in your area sits at that floor, adding them up would produce a total larger than the whole state of Georgia, which cannot be true. Those terms are shown as below the reporting threshold instead of being given a number that would not hold up.

The twelve-month plan

Four phases, sequenced by geography rather than by task, because each band of markets has to be earned before the next becomes reachable. The demand figure on each phase is what that phase opens up; the running total is everything reachable by the end of it.

Markets: Rome, Cave Spring

  • Consolidate brands., seniors. and blog.studio-kate.com into one site. The first two rank for nothing; the blog holds the only 85 rankings you own, so its content moves with a mapped redirect for every URL.
  • Rebuild the senior outfit posts on the main site. They already sit at positions 44 to 67 for terms searched around 8,100 times a month nationally.

What the data says not to do

  • Do not build a page per town for business terms. Searches pairing a town with "brand photographer", "business headshots" or "corporate headshots" measured essentially zero in all 31 markets. Those pages would target searches that do not exist, and a stack of near-identical location pages is the pattern Google treats as doorway content.
  • Do not build the strategy on "brand photographer". It is under 10 searches a month in every county you serve. It is a good description of the work and a poor way to be found.
  • Do not expect the content retainer to be found through search. Tested three separate ways, including creator-economy vocabulary, and it does not register. It is a real service people buy, just not one they search for. It gets sold in the room.
