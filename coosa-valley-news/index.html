Those three fields together are enough to open credit in someone's name. They arrive in an ordinary email inbox, and the site has no privacy policy, no contest rules, no stated odds, and no age check anywhere on it. Of everything in this audit, this is the one we'd move on first.

Every Floyd County arrest report runs a defense attorney's advertisement in the featured-image slot, with the story's own headline as its alt text. Polk County reports run a bail bondsman's. High Site tells Google to show only small preview images, which caps its chances in Google Discover

Plain English: Google Discover is the phone feed that shows people news stories they did not search for. It is a picture-first feed and a significant traffic source for local publishers. This one line of code tells Google 'do not show my big pictures.' Being precise about the consequence, because it matters: this does not remove the site from Discover, and anyone who tells you it does is overstating it. What it does is restrict stories to a small thumbnail, and in a feed where a full-width photo sits next to a thumbnail, the thumbnail loses. Google's own guidance for Discover is to use large image previews. So the site is not locked out, it is competing with one hand tied, in the one channel where the pictures are the product. It is a single line to change.

High DECISION: the site currently blocks every major AI crawler - this trades AI referral traffic for training protection, and it was probably a Cloudflare default rather than a choice Decision to make

Net position is better than it first looks: the blocks that are in place are almost entirely TRAINING blocks, and the retrieval/citation bots that actually send referral traffic (OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User, PerplexityBot, and Googlebot itself) are not blocked. So AI Overviews and ChatGPT citations remain possible today. The genuine costs are narrower: no Gemini grounding citations (Google-Extended), no presence in the Common Crawl dataset that many smaller AI products build on, and no Meta or Apple Intelligence surfacing. The genuine benefit is that the archive is not being ingested for free into model training. LiteSpeed Cache is confirmed installed via REST namespaces litespeed/v1 and litespeed/v3 and via the `x-litespeed-cache` response headers. Advanced Ads is confirmed via the advanced-ads/v1 namespace and its asset paths. Neither exposes a version string in the page source. Only the companion slider does: `advanced-ads-slider/public/assets/js/unslider.min.js?ver=2.0.2`, so Advanced Ads Slider is 2.0.2. Public vulnerability databases record authenticated Admin-level SQL injection affecting Advanced Ads core at versions up to 2.0.15, but since the installed core version is not observable we CANNOT say whether this site falls in that range, and we are not claiming it does. LiteSpeed Cache has a history of high-severity issues in past versions; again, version unknown, so no claim is made. Marked unverified deliberately.

Low Positive: WordPress core is fully patched and on the current release Opportunity

This is the most important thing to get right and you have it right. The overwhelming majority of WordPress compromises exploit known vulnerabilities in outdated core or plugins, and being current on a security release published three weeks ago puts you ahead of most small publishers. Worth stating plainly to the owners as a strength, since the rest of this report is corrective.

What we found

The RSS feed discloses the core version: curl https://coosavalleynews.com/feed/ returns `<generator>https://wordpress.org/?v=7.0.2</generator>`, corroborated by wp-includes assets served as `?ver=7.0.2` (comment-reply.min.js, wp-emoji-release.min.js, wp-mediaelement.min.js). WordPress 7.0.2 was released 17 July 2026 as a security release fixing one critical and one high-severity issue, and it is the current version as of this audit (7.1 is scheduled for 19 August 2026). Note the version is disclosed publicly via the generator tag and could be suppressed, though that is cosmetic. /readme.html and /license.txt both return 200 but, contrary to older guidance, modern WordPress readme.html does NOT contain a version number, so these files confirm WordPress is in use without leaking the version.

Low Positive: user enumeration is blocked and the Cloudflare WAF is filtering at the edge Opportunity

Several controls that are commonly missing on small news sites are working here. Attackers cannot easily harvest your editors' usernames, which is the normal first step before a password attack, and that meaningfully raises the cost of targeting your login page. The edge firewall is genuinely blocking known-bad tooling. The one honest caveat is that generic scripted access (plain curl, Python) is allowed through, so Bot Fight Mode either is not enabled or is not challenging simple automation; that is a reasonable trade for a news site that wants to stay accessible, but it is worth knowing.

What we found

User enumeration is blocked on both standard vectors: curl https://coosavalleynews.com/wp-json/wp/v2/users returns 401, and curl 'https://coosavalleynews.com/?author=1' returns 404 with a zero-byte body. Directory listing is off: /wp-includes/ and /wp-content/uploads/ both return 404. WAF behavior was probed by varying ONLY the User-Agent header on an ordinary GET of the homepage, with no payload of any kind: a scanner-style UA (`sqlmap/1.7`) returns 403 with a 17-byte text/plain body, `server: cloudflare`, and NO `x-powered-by` or `x-turbo-charged-by` headers, proving it was blocked at Cloudflare's edge before reaching your server. Ordinary browser and generic automation UAs (curl/8.5.0, python-requests/2.31.0) return 200. TLS is valid and modern: Google Trust Services WE1 issuer, ECDSA P-256 key, SAN covering coosavalleynews.com and *.coosavalleynews.com, valid 14 Jun 2026 to 12 Sep 2026, negotiating TLS 1.3 with X25519. http:// correctly 301-redirects to https://.

Where this goes

What we would do next

Deliberately kept at a high level. The detailed sequencing is a conversation to have once you have decided which of these you actually want to take on.

  1. Settle the two questions only you can answer

    Two findings in here are choices, not defects. Whether to keep blocking AI assistants from reading the site, and whether syndicated entertainment and travel filler belongs on a local news domain. Both change what the rest of the plan should look like, so they come first.

  2. Close the trust and legal gaps

    The missing policy pages, the single shared byline, and the arrest-report exposure sit together. They are the cheapest items on this list to fix and they are the ones capping how far the rest can carry you.

  3. Fix the plumbing that is throttling reach

    Image weight, the caching rules, the ad-slot heights, the Discover setting and the structured data. None of it is a redesign. It is configuration on top of publishing you are already doing well.

  4. Measure it, then re-run this exact report

    Baseline the real numbers in Search Console first, then re-run this audit at 30, 60 and 90 days so the movement is visible rather than asserted.

How this was measured

Everything in this report came from the public website on 5 August 2026, using the same requests a search engine or a reader's browser makes. No login, no plugin, no access to your analytics, hosting or ad platform was used or needed. Page weights were measured by downloading every file on the page and adding up the real transferred bytes. Image savings were confirmed by actually re-encoding your images, not estimated. Post counts came from your own sitemap.
