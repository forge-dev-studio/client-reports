How the traffic is valued

Search visits are priced the way an advertiser would pay for them. Each non-brand visit is valued at $4.28, weighted across the searches actually sending visitors to the site rather than across the wider category, which keeps the rate tied to real traffic. Searches for the company name are removed first, since those visitors are already looking for S&S by name. That leaves 631 of the 717 visits in the valuation.

Stated as a range. Google reports the exact search behind roughly a quarter of visits and groups the rest, so the blended rate carries real uncertainty. At $3.50 to $5.50 per visit the traffic values at $2,208 to $3,470, with $2,701 as the central figure. Web search only: visits and calls arriving through the Google Business Profile map listing sit on a separate surface and are not counted.

How this was measured

Search visits, appearances, and average position come from Google Search Console for April 16 to August 19, 2026. Advertising rates come from Google Ads keyword data. Lead counts come from the contact records created by the website form and were verified against those records directly. Company-name searches are excluded from the traffic valuation. April begins on the 16th, the first day of search reporting, and August covers the first nineteen days.

Three things are worth stating alongside the figures. Google groups the exact search term behind most visits, so the blended visit rate is an estimate carried as a range. Average position moves with the mix of searches a site appears for, so it reads differently as page coverage widens. And leads are counted from every route into the website, which includes the map listing and direct visits, not the search visits alone.
