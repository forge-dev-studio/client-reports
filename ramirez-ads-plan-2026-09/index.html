What held the account back

  • Most of the money went far from home. Four metro Atlanta targets (Atlanta, Fulton County, Cobb County and Marietta) took 73% of the Search clicks and $2,224 of the $3,326 spent on Search. Atlanta alone took half the clicks. Bartow County got 2 clicks in seven months, Cartersville 7.

The Search campaign was set to let Google bid automatically for calls, and with almost nothing counted, it had nothing to learn from. Neither did you.

  • The call button never ran on Search. The Search campaign's call extension didn't serve once, so phone-first customers only saw a link to the website.

One Google Search campaign. Search ads show to people typing what they want into Google, which is the moment someone is looking for a builder. How it spends

We set the top price for each keyword ourselves instead of letting Google decide. People ready to hire, searching things like "deck builder near me" or "fence company near me", get the most room. We block searches that aren't customers: price-only and do-it-yourself searches, lumber and supplies, job seekers, rentals, and the card games that also use the words "deck builder".

The money splits about 45% fences, 45% decks, 10% deck repair and walls. Fences are more than half of the searches in your towns, and your profile doesn't show in the map results for fence searches yet, so ads are where fence customers will find you first.

Why $20 a day

  • It reaches most of the people actually searching. There are about 1,500 searches a month for these services in your 20 towns. At $20 a day we expect the ads to show for most of the ones that matter.
  • The extra leads still cost a sensible amount. Going from $10 to $20 a day, each added lead costs about $220. Past $20, each added lead costs $540 or more, because the extra money mostly raises the price of the same clicks.
  • We learn faster. Close to twice the leads in the first three months gives us, and Google, more to judge the campaign by.

If you'd rather start at $10 a day, the plan stays the same. It just shows for fewer searches, and the 30-day review tells us whether to move up.

What that could mean in jobs

Leads aren't jobs. As an example only: if you win about 1 in 5 of the people who reach out, $20 a day points to roughly one job every one to two months from ads, and $10 a day to roughly one every two to three months. Tell us how many of your estimates usually turn into jobs, and your average job size, and we'll put real numbers here.

What the estimates assume

  • Searches: about 1,500 a month in your 20 towns for the keywords in this plan. Fences about 830, deck building about 540, deck repair about 80, retaining walls about 80. Our estimate from Google keyword data for the Atlanta and Chattanooga markets, scaled to the share of people who live in your towns.
  • Click price: about $10 to $16 on average, depending on budget. Google's own planner lists $16 to $22 for these searches; we bid below that and accept fewer clicks.
  • Lead rate: 5% to 9% of clicks become a form or a call of a minute or longer. Your account has never measured this, so it's the biggest unknown. A local service business we manage measured about 8% from calls alone.
  • Season: searches for outdoor projects move with the weather, so any single month can land above or below these ranges.
  • Answering: the ranges assume calls get answered or returned quickly. If calls go unanswered, a bigger budget just buys more missed calls.

Launch and the first 30 days

Before any money is spent

  1. You pick the daily budget. $20 is our recommendation.
  2. We set up call counting. The Google forwarding number, with recording off unless you want it on.
  3. We connect Google Analytics to Google Ads, with personalized advertising turned off, so both tools see the same leads.
  4. We test together. One test form and one test call, to confirm every lead reaches your phone, your email and your customer records.
  5. We build the campaign, you look over the ads, and it goes live.

After it's live

Week 1
We read the actual searches every few days and block anything that isn't a customer.
Day 14
We see when calls really connect and set the call button to those hours; outside them, the ad points to the form. We rebalance money between decks and fences.
Day 21
We replace the weakest ad lines with new ones.
Day 30
We go over the real numbers with you: cost per click, how often the ads showed, leads, and which leads became jobs.

At day 30, one of three moves

  • Leads cost about what we expected and the budget runs out early in the day: go to $25 to $30 a day and add Bartow, Gordon and Floyd counties, your home counties and the least expensive new searches. Only with your OK.
  • The ads show too rarely because other advertisers outbid us: raise bids on the best keywords rather than the budget.
  • Leads cost more than expected: tighten keywords and towns before spending more.

Next, if you want it: Local Services Ads

These are the boxes at the very top of Google, above the regular ads, and you pay per lead instead of per click. Google offers them for decking and for fencing. To join, Google asks for proof of liability insurance and runs background checks, which takes about three to four weeks. If you have a current insurance certificate, send it over and we'll start the application alongside this campaign.
