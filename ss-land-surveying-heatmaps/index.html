The figures above are for land surveyor, remeasured on September 3 against the real results page. The map pack is strongest close to home and stays strong further out than expected: S&S is in it at every point within ten miles and at 25 of the 26 points between ten and twenty miles, which is why it comes up second in Rome. It thins out through the twenties and is gone by forty miles.

Across all five terms the firm took a pack position 225 times, and 144 of those were first place. The ordinary listings behave differently: weaker close to home, but still turning up out at thirty and forty miles where the pack has stopped. The two surfaces cover different ground, which is why both matter.

Ordinary search results behave the opposite way. They start slower close to home, then hold steady out across the whole region, fading gradually instead of cutting off. Of the 134 points tested, 49 are places where the website is visible and the Maps listing is not. That is ground the business only reaches because of the website.

It matters because of where the work actually comes from. The typical inquiry travels well beyond ten miles, so the map listing alone would never have reached most of the customers who found the firm this year.

Where S&S does rank, it ranks high. Half of all its placements across the grid are in the top three. On the plain term land surveyor it comes up first at the office, second in Rome, first near Cartersville and fourth as far out as Carrollton. The narrow point count for that term is a reflection of how few places it appears at all, not of where it sits when it does.

Wording changes the result more than distance does. Anything phrased as near me reaches far more of the map than the bare terms do.

One row needs a correction. These maps read the ordinary search listings only, so a business that appears in the block of businesses beside the map, rather than in the blue links underneath, reads as absent here. Land surveying is exactly that case. It shows as zero above, but Search Console records the firm at an average position of 1.3 on that exact search across 86 appearances in August. It is one of the strongest terms the firm has, not a missing one. Treat the zero as a limit of this particular measurement.

How these were measured. Every point on every map is a real Google search run from that map coordinate, not an estimate. The search results columns come from ordinary Google results read twenty deep. The Maps columns come from the Google Maps index, which is related to, but not identical with, the three businesses shown beside the map on a results page, so read the Maps side as directional. The search results side reads the ordinary listings twenty deep and does not capture the block of businesses shown beside the map, which is why a strong local term can read as a zero here. The 40 mile study used 134 points at 5 mile spacing across 5 keyword variations. The 60 mile study used 141 points at 7 mile spacing. Results reflect the day they were captured, August 24, 2026, and rankings move over time.
