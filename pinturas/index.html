What this measures

When somebody in this area needs a painter, they type something into Google. This report checks 40 of those searches, 20 in English and 20 in Spanish, across 13 towns inside a 30 mile radius of Rome. That is 513 individual checks.

For each one we recorded where Pinturas landed in Google's local results, the list that sits under the map. Position 1, 2 or 3 is what most people actually see without scrolling or tapping for more. Anything past that is real, but far fewer people ever look at it.

Two things worth stepping back from

The Spanish lead is real, and the Spanish audience is small

Google's own advertising data puts Georgia demand for these Spanish searches at roughly 350 a month against roughly 36,110 a month for the English equivalents. Pinturas is winning a lane with very little traffic in it, and is largely absent from the lane that carries almost all of the volume. Both facts are true at once, and the second one is where the growth is.

AI answers barely exist in this trade yet

Google produced an AI answer on 5 of the 60 searches checked, and named Pinturas in 0 of them. That surface is small today. It is worth knowing that the Pinturas website currently gives an AI engine nothing to read: no business details in a machine readable form, no review data, and no text that survives without scripts running.

What we would do next

  1. Confirm which towns are worth winning

    Rome is already strong. The question is which of the other 12 towns are genuinely worth competing for, based on the work Pinturas wants more of.

  2. Build the website around those markets and both languages

    The current site gives Google and every AI engine almost nothing to work with, in either language.

  3. Ongoing optimization and reputation work

    Keeping the profile, the listings and the review flow working together as the map results shift.

  4. Re-run this exact report at 30, 60 and 90 days, then monthly

    Same searches, same towns, same method, so any change is measured rather than assumed.
